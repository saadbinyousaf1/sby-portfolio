HighLevel Workflow Maps: See Every Automation in One Map

HighLevel's new Workflow Maps draws a live map of every workflow and what it touches. What it shows, who gets it, and five jobs it makes easier.

The short version

Workflow Maps is a new view inside HighLevel's Automation area. It reads every workflow in a sub-account and draws them as a map: workflows grouped in their folders, with the tags, forms, calendars, pipelines and custom fields they use arranged around them and joined by lines.

People describe it as a mind map for automations, and that's a fair picture. The difference from a diagram you'd draw yourself is that HighLevel builds it from the real workflows, so it can't go out of date.

It was introduced around HighLevel's LevelUp event on 27 October 2026.

The problem it solves

A HighLevel account rarely has one workflow. After a few months it has twenty or forty, and they depend on each other in ways nobody wrote down. A form starts one workflow. That workflow adds a tag. The tag starts a second workflow, which moves a pipeline stage, which starts a third.

Until now the only way to understand a chain like that was to open each workflow in turn and keep the picture in your head. That's slow in your own account and painful in a client's account you've just inherited. It's also how accounts break: someone renames a tag or deletes a form without knowing three workflows were waiting on it.

Where to find it and who gets it

Open Automation > Workflows > Workflow Maps. You can also open Maps from inside a workflow's builder. A first-visit introduction explains the controls, and a Help button on the map opens walkthroughs.

Check your access before you plan around itHighLevel's two official sources describe access differently. The changelog says launch access is limited to agency users and agency admins. The LevelUp 2026 post lists Workflow Maps under the Founder plan. If you can't see it in your account, that is the likely reason, and access may widen over time.

Five jobs it makes easier

1.Taking over an account you didn't build

Open the map before you open a single workflow. In a couple of minutes you can see how many workflows exist, which folders matter, and which forms and tags sit at the centre of everything.

2.Checking what depends on something before you change it

About to rename a tag, replace a form or retire a calendar? Click the asset on the map. The panel lists every workflow that uses it, so you know what to update first.

3.Answering "why did this customer get that message?"

Search for the contact and open their journey. The timeline shows which workflows they entered and what happened in each. This used to mean checking execution logs workflow by workflow.

4.Catching failures before a client does

Use the errors view as a weekly check. A failed step can mean a lead never got a reply. The map gathers recent failures across the whole sub-account, grouped into published and draft workflows, and you can mark them as read once reviewed.

5.Tidying up

Run Cleanup. Workflows still called "New Workflow", workflows nobody has entered in a long time and empty folders are the clutter that makes accounts hard to hand over.

What it doesn't do

  • It isn't a builder. You still edit steps in the normal workflow builder. The map opens it for you.
  • It works one sub-account at a time. There is no agency-wide map across all clients.
  • Parts are still marked preview. Asset usage and contact journeys carry that label in the announcement, so expect changes.
  • It won't fix bad structure. The map makes a tangled account visible. Untangling it is still your job.

Is it a reason to upgrade?

For a single business with five workflows, no. You can hold five workflows in your head.

For an agency maintaining dozens of client accounts, it is one of the more useful things HighLevel has shipped for day-to-day operations. The value is in time saved on audits and in changes that don't break something downstream. If you're already weighing the Founder plan for SaaS Mode or rebilling, this adds to the case. On its own it is a thin reason to pay $200 a month more. The pricing guide covers what else that plan includes.

Common questions

What is Workflow Maps in HighLevel?

Workflow Maps is a visual interface that shows a sub-account's workflows, folders and connected assets such as tags, forms, calendars and pipelines, with the links between them. HighLevel generates it from your existing workflows, so there is nothing to draw or maintain.

Where do I find Workflow Maps?

Go to Automation, then Workflows, then Workflow Maps. You can also open Maps from inside a workflow's builder.

Is Workflow Maps available on every plan?

Not at launch. HighLevel's changelog says launch access is limited to agency users and agency admins, and its LevelUp 2026 post lists Workflow Maps under the Founder plan. Check your own account to see whether it has been enabled.

Can I edit workflows from the map?

The map is for seeing, tracing and troubleshooting. To change a step you open the workflow in the normal builder, which the map links to.

Does each sub-account have its own map?

Yes. HighLevel says each sub-account has its own map.
