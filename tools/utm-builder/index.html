Guide

What UTM parameters are

UTM parameters are short tags added to the end of a link that say where a visitor came from. When someone clicks yourbusiness.com/book?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=spring-promo, the page they land on can read those tags and record that the visit came from your spring promotion on Facebook.

They don't change the page. Visitors land in the same place either way. The tags simply travel along with the click so your tools can attribute the lead correctly, instead of filing it under “direct” or “unknown”.

ParameterAnswersExample
utm_sourceWhich site or platform?facebook, google, newsletter
utm_mediumWhat type of channel?paid_social, cpc, email, text
utm_campaignWhich promotion?spring-whitening-2026
utm_contentWhich ad or link?video-ad-a, header-button
utm_termWhich keyword or audience?teeth-whitening

Source, medium and campaign are the essentials. Content and term are optional but useful when you test several ads or keywords in the same campaign.

How HighLevel uses UTM data

When a visitor arrives on a HighLevel funnel or website with UTM tags and then submits a form, survey or booking, HighLevel records the source information on the new contact. You can see it in the contact's attribution details, usually as the first touch that brought them in and the latest touch before they converted.

That attribution flows into reporting, so you can see which channels and campaigns actually produce leads and booked appointments rather than just clicks. It also means you can build smart lists and workflows on it, for example sending leads from a specific campaign into their own follow-up sequence.

Attribution only works if the tags are on the link people actually click. A beautifully configured CRM can't tell a Facebook lead from a Google one if the ad points to a bare URL.

A naming convention that keeps reports clean

UTM values are case-sensitive and literal. Facebook, facebook and fb show up as three different sources. Most messy attribution reports come down to inconsistent naming, not missing tags. Pick a convention and stick to it:

  • Always lowercase. The “Clean up values” option above does this for you.
  • Hyphens instead of spaces — spring-promo, not spring promo or spring%20promo.
  • Use the presets for source and medium so every team member tags the same channel the same way.
  • Put the date or season in the campaign name, like black-friday-2026, so next year's campaign doesn't merge with this one.
  • Write it down. Keep a shared list of the names you use. The history panel above helps, but it only lives in your browser.

Tagging by channel

Facebook and Instagram ads

Paste the Parameters only output into the ad's URL parameters field in Meta Ads Manager rather than editing the website URL. Turn on Meta dynamic values and Meta fills in the real campaign, ad set and ad names when the ad is clicked, so you don't have to tag each ad by hand.

Google Ads

Google Ads can track clicks automatically with its own click ID, but adding UTM tags as well means the source also reaches HighLevel. Add them in the final URL suffix or the tracking template at campaign level.

Email and SMS

Tag every link in a broadcast with the email or SMS preset and a campaign name for that send. For texts, watch the length: a long tagged URL can use most of a 160-character segment. A HighLevel trigger link or a short path on your own domain keeps texts compact. The SMS segment calculator shows exactly what a link costs.

Print and QR codes

Offline is where attribution is usually lost. Tag the link with the QR preset, generate the QR code here, and print it on flyers, yard signs or vehicle wraps. Leads from the flyer then show up as their own source instead of “direct”.

Common mistakes

  • Tagging internal links. UTMs are for links from outside your site. Tagging links between your own pages overwrites the real source.
  • Putting tags after the # in a URL. Anything after a hash isn't sent to the page. This builder always places the tags before it.
  • Tagging a shortened link instead of the real landing page.
  • Forgetting the medium. Without it, many reports can't group the traffic by channel.
  • Sharing a tagged link publicly for something other than its campaign, such as copying an ad link into a social post. The visits will be credited to the ad.

Frequently asked questions

Which UTM parameters are required?
utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign are the core three that most tools expect. utm_content and utm_term are optional and help when you are comparing several ads or keywords within one campaign.
Do UTM parameters affect SEO?
Not when they are used on links from ads, emails, texts and other sites. Avoid adding them to links between pages on your own website, because that creates duplicate URLs and overwrites the real source of the visit.
Does HighLevel track UTM parameters?
Yes. When a tagged visitor submits a form, survey or booking on a HighLevel funnel or website, the source information is saved to the contact's attribution, which you can use in reports, smart lists and workflows.
Should UTM values be lowercase?
Yes. UTM values are case-sensitive, so Facebook and facebook are counted as two sources. Using lowercase with hyphens keeps reports consistent.
What are Meta dynamic URL parameters?
Placeholders such as {{campaign.name}}, {{adset.name}} and {{ad.name}} that Meta Ads Manager replaces with the real names when someone clicks your ad. They save you tagging each ad by hand.
Where is my link history stored?
Only in your own browser's local storage. It is not sent anywhere, and clearing your browser data or using another device will not show it.
