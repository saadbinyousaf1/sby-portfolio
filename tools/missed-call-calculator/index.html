Why missed calls cost more than they look

For most service businesses, a phone call is the highest-intent lead there is. Someone who calls has already decided they need the service and is ready to talk now. When that call goes unanswered, many callers don't leave a voicemail. They hang up and call the next business on the list.

The problem is that the loss is invisible. Nobody records the customer they never had. A missed call shows up as a line in the call log, not as lost revenue, so it rarely gets the attention a bad review or a lost deal would.

This calculator puts a number on it, using your own call volume, close rate and customer value. The result is an estimate, not a forecast, but it is usually enough to show whether fixing missed calls deserves an hour of your time this week.

How the calculation works

The math is deliberately simple, so you can check it and change any assumption:

  1. Missed calls = inbound calls × the share that go unanswered.
  2. Missed new leads = missed calls × the share that are new potential customers. The rest are existing customers, suppliers, wrong numbers and spam, who usually call back.
  3. Customers lost = missed new leads × your normal close rate. We only count the ones you would have won if you had answered.
  4. Lost revenue = customers lost × average customer value, then × 12 for the year.
  5. Recovered = lost revenue × the share an instant text-back wins back.

The industry presets are only starting points to make the tool usable straight away. They are not benchmarks. Replace every number with figures from your own call reports and sales records for an answer that means something.

Where to find your real numbers

  • Calls and missed calls: your phone system's call log. In HighLevel, the call reporting and conversation history show answered, missed and voicemail calls for numbers on the account.
  • New-lead share: sample 30 missed calls and check how many numbers were not already contacts.
  • Close rate and value: your pipeline reports, or a rough count of quotes sent against jobs won last quarter.

What a missed-call text-back does

A missed-call text-back sends an automatic SMS to anyone whose call you couldn't answer, usually within a minute. Instead of hearing nothing, the caller gets a message from your business asking how you can help.

It works because it catches people at the moment they are still looking. Many callers who would never leave a voicemail will happily reply to a text, and a reply opens a conversation your team can pick up as soon as they are free. The 30% recovery default is a conservative assumption, not a promise. Track your own reply rate for a month and update it.

How to set up a missed-call text-back in HighLevel

The usual way is a workflow. Menu names can shift between HighLevel updates, but the structure stays the same:

  1. Create a workflow and add a call trigger, such as Call Status, filtered to missed or unanswered inbound calls. Some accounts also offer a built-in missed-call text-back setting in the phone number options.
  2. Add a Send SMS action with one of the templates above. Keep it short, name your business, and ask an open question.
  3. Notify your team with an internal notification or a task, so a person follows up when the caller replies.
  4. Optional: add a booking link as a custom value, like {{custom_values.booking_link}}, so callers can book without waiting for a reply.
  5. Test it by calling the number from your own phone and letting it ring out.

Your phone number needs an approved A2P 10DLC campaign that covers this kind of message before texts will deliver reliably. The A2P 10DLC kit writes the registration copy for you.

Text-back best practices

  • Speed matters most. Send it immediately. A text that arrives an hour later is just a callback by text.
  • Sound human. “Sorry we missed your call! How can we help?” gets more replies than a formal notice.
  • Name the business so the caller knows who is texting.
  • Don't send it to everyone. Exclude existing customers with open jobs, or use a different message for them, if your workflow can tell them apart.
  • Close the loop. A reply that sits unanswered is worse than no text at all. Route replies to someone who will respond the same day.

Using this calculator in a sales call

If you set up HighLevel for clients, this is one of the easiest ways to show value in the first meeting. Ask the prospect three questions: how many calls they get, roughly how many they miss, and what a new customer is worth. Fill it in live, and let them adjust the numbers they disagree with.

Then set the monthly cost to your fee and the return badge shows the multiple. Because the prospect chose the inputs, the result is theirs, not your pitch.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate the cost of missed calls?
Multiply your missed calls by the share that are new leads, then by your close rate and your average customer value. That gives lost revenue per month. Multiply by 12 for the year. The calculator does this for you and shows each step.
What percentage of calls do small businesses miss?
It varies widely with staffing, opening hours and call volume. Rather than relying on a general figure, check your own call log for a typical month. The presets use example values only so the tool works out of the box.
Does a missed-call text-back really work?
It gives callers an easy way to continue the conversation, which many prefer to leaving a voicemail. Results depend on how fast it is sent, how it is worded and how quickly your team responds to replies. Track your own reply rate and adjust the recovery assumption.
Can I set up missed-call text-back in HighLevel?
Yes. The common approach is a workflow with a call trigger filtered to missed calls and a Send SMS action, plus a notification so your team follows up on replies. Your number needs an approved A2P 10DLC campaign for texts to deliver reliably.
Should I use first-job value or lifetime value?
Use first-job value for a conservative estimate. If customers usually come back, such as for dental cleanings or recurring service, lifetime value is a fairer measure of what a lost customer costs. Try both to see the range.
Are my numbers saved anywhere?
No. Everything is calculated in your browser. Your last inputs are remembered only in your browser's local storage for convenience.
